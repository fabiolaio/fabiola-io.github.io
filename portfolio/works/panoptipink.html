Panoptipink

Installation with pink bedazzled CRT TVs, surveillance cameras, LEDs, live video feeds
Bakehouse Art Complex, Miami, 2025

Panoptipink investigates how surveillance has become normalized in everyday life. Using the visual language of Y2K—pink, rhinestones, and cuteness—I want to explore how surveillance systems, often disguised as friendly and helpful, influence our behavior and identity.

The installation draws on Jeremy Bentham’s panopticon model but inverted, where constant observation creates internalized control, so people will act differently like if they are seeing themselves in a mirror, they are going to perform for themselves.

By placing the spectator at the center of a circular arrangement of nine pink CRT TVs displaying live surveillance feeds, the work makes viewers aware of their own visibility. The bedazzled TVs, with their cute aesthetic, contrast sharply with the serious theme, highlighting how today’s digital surveillance systems are often embedded in entertainment and social media platforms, blurring the line between being watched and performing for an audience.

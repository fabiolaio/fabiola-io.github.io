Working Inside the Machine

Notes on AI, Surveillance, and Artistic Practice

Fabiola Larios · October 2026

I grew up with the internet. I remember dial-up, the excitement of finding strange corners of the web, and the feeling that online spaces could be places to experiment with who we were. Today, much of that same space runs on tracking, prediction, and the collection of personal information.

That transformation is part of why I make art about technology. I am not interested in declaring myself for or against artificial intelligence. I want to know what these systems do, what they make visible, and what they hide.

In Wild Wired World, I use AI-generated imagery to revisit the history of the internet. In Cuteveillance, I combine generated images and YOLOv7 object detection to turn surveillance cameras into the very things being watched and classified. These are not attempts to make technology innocent. They are ways of working through its contradictions.

Sometimes my materials are screens and algorithms. Sometimes they are obsolete computers, cameras, rhinestones, or electronic waste. Each carries a history: of desire, of production, of use, and eventually of disposal. The digital world has a physical body, even when its interfaces encourage us to forget that.

I do not think an artist has to stand outside a system to question it. Most of us already live inside these systems. I would rather use them, interrupt them, decorate them, and look closely at what happens.

I am not trying to make technology innocent. I am trying to make its mechanisms visible.
